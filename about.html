About / Nelvin Mwendwa

Still figuring it out.
Building anyway.

I don't have a twenty-year software story. I'm a student building that story one serious project at a time.

I'm studying Computer Science at the Cooperative University of Kenya. My practical interest has settled around Python, automation and backend systems — especially when the software has to deal with real data rather than a toy example.

The Compliance Intelligence Platform came from a simple problem: public records are useful, but they're often scattered across institutional pages and difficult to search consistently. I built a pipeline to collect, normalize and cite those records, then exposed the result through an API.

Another project took me in the opposite direction: tick-by-tick market data and a Deriv trading bot. That work taught me a different lesson — a system can execute perfectly and still be a bad system if the assumptions, risk controls and evidence behind the strategy aren't solid.

Those two projects look unrelated on the surface. They aren't. Both force me to care about data quality, reproducibility, failure cases and what happens after the demo works.

How I learn

I tend to learn by building, breaking, investigating and rebuilding. When something interests me, I want to understand the machinery underneath it — not just enough to make the interface look right.

University gives me the foundations; personal projects give me somewhere to test them. I'm still early in the journey, and that's deliberate: I'm more interested in becoming genuinely capable than in pretending I've already arrived.

Outside the repository

I like long-form reading, difficult questions and arguments that make me reconsider an assumption. The same curiosity that keeps me inside a debugging session longer than planned also shows up away from code.
