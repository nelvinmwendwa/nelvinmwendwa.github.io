Case study / Software quality assurance

Testing the workflows
behind an ERP/POS.

A structured functional QA engagement on a pre-launch business management and point-of-sale system. The focus was reproducible behaviour, clear defect evidence and practical launch-readiness observations.

Areas exercised

Settings, inventory, vendors, purchases, customers, point of sale, sales history, users and branches, accounting, tax management, reports and dashboard.

Representative findings

Examples are summarised from the defect register; no tenant URLs, credentials, customer records or screenshots are published here.

Inventory and stock integrity

  • Inventory valuation appeared to use selling price rather than cost price.
  • Stock adjustment and bulk-update workflows accepted quantities that drove stock below zero.
  • Adjustment type did not consistently enforce the expected stock direction.
  • Some non-main-branch adjustments returned an unexplained 403 response.

Sales, customers and accounting

  • Refunds reversed stock and financial totals in some workflows but did not consistently reverse customer points and spend.
  • Customer tier discounts appeared in the cart but were not applied to the completed sale.
  • Profit figures differed between the dashboard, overview and profit-and-loss views.
  • Journal, cash-flow and balance-sheet outputs showed inconsistencies requiring product-owner review.

Tax, reports and usability

  • VAT calculations did not consistently equal 16% of the taxable amount in the tested scenario.
  • Export behaviour differed by module; some exports failed while report CSV downloads worked.
  • Some screens showed incomplete status information or unclear feedback for failed operations.

Launch-readiness observations

These were visible during normal functional use and are not claims that a penetration test was performed.

  • The login page was served over HTTP rather than HTTPS at the time of testing.
  • Test accounts used weak/shared passwords; unique credentials and a stronger policy were recommended.
  • Two-factor authentication was shown as disabled in the account summary.
  • Email receipt delivery was not configured; SMS, WhatsApp and M-Pesa flows were not verified because a safe test mode had not been confirmed.
  • Tax/e-receipt compliance indicators and placeholder-looking identifiers required confirmation by the product owner.
  • Card-entry guidance required review in favour of a hosted payment-gateway flow that avoids handling raw card details.

Method and boundaries

Testing was performed as functional QA using a tenant-level test account and synthetic records. Test cases captured preconditions, steps, expected outcomes, actual outcomes and status. Reproducible mismatches were linked to defect IDs in the register.

Security testing, penetration testing, destructive testing against production data, cross-tenant probing and real-payment execution were not part of this work. Payment and messaging integrations were not treated as verified without an approved sandbox or mock mode.

Scope note: This case study describes QA work and observed behaviour. It does not imply that I developed BizFlow, fixed the logged defects, approved its compliance status or certified it for production release.
